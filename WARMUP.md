# 整理了一些和同学讲解过程中的抽象对话！
Warm-up: Explaining player Chinese to a non-Chinese-speaking colleague

## 1. 策划是懂玩家的，懂得怎么悄悄改概率
Me: Literally it sounds like, “The designers really understand players — they even know how to quietly change the rates.”
Colleague: Wait, so that’s praise?
Me: No, completely the opposite. It’s sarcasm. The player is basically saying, “Yeah, they understand us so well that they know exactly how to secretly make the odds worse.”
Colleague: Ah, so more like, “Great job, you really know how to screw us over.”
Me: Exactly. The real signal is distrust, especially around whether the gacha rates are fair.

## 2. 这波属于是逼氪了，懂的都懂
Me: “逼氪” literally means being pushed or forced to spend money.
Colleague: Like pay-to-win?
Me: Similar, but not exactly. It’s more like players feel the game has deliberately created pressure to make them pay — maybe through a banner, a character, or a mechanic.
Colleague: And “懂的都懂”?
Me: That’s basically, “If you know, you know.” It makes it sound like everyone in the community already understands what the company is doing.

## 3. 艾琳 = 铁锅，新人别懵
Colleague: Why is Aileen an iron wok?
Me: That’s exactly the point — if you’re new, it makes no sense. “铁锅” is just a community nickname for Aileen.
Colleague: Where did the nickname come from?
Me: From this sentence alone, we actually can’t know. I’d need to look at the surrounding messages instead of inventing an explanation.
Colleague: So it’s like when a fandom gives a character some random nickname and everyone just starts using it?
Me: Yes. This message is basically helping newcomers decode an inside joke.

## 4. 白嫖党表示毫无压力 [doge]
Me: “白嫖党” means players who spend no money and try to get everything for free.
Colleague: So, “Free-to-play players have no pressure”?
Me: On the surface, yes. But the [doge] makes it suspicious.
Colleague: Like a sarcasm emoji?
Me: Pretty much. It could mean, “Yeah, absolutely no pressure… obviously,” or the player is making fun of themselves for avoiding the whole problem by not spending.
Colleague: So you wouldn’t label that as positive sentiment.
Me: Exactly.

## 5. 非酋报道，160抽一个金都没有，绷不住了
Colleague: I recognise 160 pulls, but what’s “非酋”?
Me: A very unlucky player. Basically the opposite of an “欧皇”, someone with amazing luck.
Colleague: And “一个金都没有”?
Me: No top-rarity pull at all.
Colleague: So after 160 pulls they got nothing good?
Me: Right. And “绷不住了” is like, “I can’t take this anymore” or “I’m losing it.”
Colleague: So it’s joking language, but the frustration is actually very real.
Me: Exactly. That mix of humour and frustration is very common in Chinese gaming communities.
